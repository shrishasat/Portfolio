02 SEPTEMBER 2026

The sequel that reads whole sentences

How much better can a mind-reading keyboard get in eighteen months? On 29 June 2026, Meta AI released Brain2Qwerty v2 alongside the peer-reviewed paper for v1 — timing that felt deliberate. V2 reasons over words and meaning instead of single characters, trained on around 22,000 sentences from nine volunteers wearing MEG helmets for hours at a time.

Meta open-sourced the training code for both versions. How much of the remaining gap is engineering, and how much is something we haven't named yet?

Source: AI at Meta, Brain2Qwerty v2 announcement.
2025
28 JUNE 2025

Typing with your mind, one letter at a time

Can you type without touching a keyboard? In February 2025, Meta AI unveiled Brain2Qwerty — a system that reads EEG or MEG signals while someone silently types on a QWERTY layout, then a language model cleans up the guesses.

MEG achieved substantially better character decoding than EEG in their experiments. Still a laboratory system rather than a laptop. What happens when the hardware finally catches up to the model?

Source: Meta AI, Brain2Qwerty.
2024
03 MARCH 2024

Can a machine hear what you're only listening to?

What does it mean for an algorithm to eavesdrop on perception itself? In 2023, Meta AI described a model that matched short segments of MEG or EEG activity to spoken sentences.

No implants. No conscious button press. Just neural activity and a model trying to infer which linguistic representation was being experienced.

Source: Défossez et al., Nature Machine Intelligence.

18 NOVEMBER 2019

Why does a continent burn before summer even starts?

In November 2019, bushfires tore through New South Wales and Queensland weeks ahead of the usual season, turning the sky over Sydney the colour of rust.

I kept coming back to one image: koalas moving faster than the smoke. It made me wonder how any brain — human or animal — decides, in half a second, whether to freeze or flee.
